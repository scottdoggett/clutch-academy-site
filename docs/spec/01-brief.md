# 01 — Brief

Business context, audience, and positioning. This is the "why" behind the copy
decisions everywhere else; it changes rarely.

## The business

**Clutch Academy** teaches manual (stick shift, standard) transmission driving
in Toronto, Ontario. One instructor — **Sam Anthony**, founder and lead
instructor — teaching already-licensed drivers to operate a manual car, on real
roads, one-on-one.

Lessons are taught in a manual 2015 Volkswagen Golf. A valid **G2 or G licence
is required** for every lesson; Clutch Academy is not an MTO beginner-driver
school and does not teach unlicensed learners.

## Goals, in priority order

1. **Drive bookings via Calendly.** Every page funnels to a Book CTA.
2. **Rank.** The rebuild exists because the single-page site had one URL to
   compete with. Dedicated crawlable pages per package are the whole point.
3. **Build trust.** Prospects are handing a nervous experience to a stranger.
   About, reviews, and testimonials carry that weight.
4. **Communicate pricing clearly.** Transparent upfront pricing differentiates
   against consultation-based competitors.

Goals 2 and 3 both serve goal 1; when they conflict, bookings win.

## Audience

Licensed **G2 or G** drivers in the GTA, roughly 17–35, skewing male but not
exclusively.

**Motivations, in order of emphasis:**

1. **Renting a manual car on a European trip.** Specifically flagged by the
   client to emphasise — many European rentals are manual-only or materially
   cheaper in manual. This shows up as its own scenario card on
   `/lessons/individual`.
2. Enthusiast interest — car culture, sports cars.
3. Owning, or about to own, a manual car.
4. The skill for its own sake.

**In the audience:** licensed drivers who've never touched a clutch; drivers
who learned on automatic and want to refresh; travellers preparing for Europe.

**Not in the audience:** unlicensed learners, fleet/commercial drivers, minors.

## Positioning

**Instructor-led.** Patience, professionalism, and one-on-one attention are the
hooks — the same pattern as GTA competitors where the instructor's personality
carries the brand. The practical implication: About is prominent, personal, and
photo-forward, and Sam's own voice is used wherever he has supplied words.

What to reinforce:

- One-on-one, patient, judgment-free instruction
- Real Toronto roads, not a parking lot for an hour
- Transparent upfront pricing
- Toronto-based, against largely suburban competitors

A small but established GTA market. Prices below were accurate when surveyed in

| StickGTA (Toronto) | High volume, reviews, gift certificates | $85 / $110 / $135 |
| Shifters (Etobicoke) | Premium coaching, dual-control car | Consultation-based |

At $109 per 75-minute lesson, Clutch Academy sits mid-market — above StickGTA's
entry rate, well below the motorsport schools.

## Brand voice

Calm, plain, and specific. The site talks the way a good instructor talks to a
nervous student.

**Do:**
- Name the fear directly, then defuse it ("You will stall — everyone does").
- Use concrete detail over adjectives: bite point, hill starts, rush hour.
- Keep sentences short. Let a hard sentence stand alone.
- Say "stick shift" and "standard" as well as "manual" — real people search all
  three, and the FAQ addresses it explicitly.

**Don't:**
- Hype. No "revolutionary", no exclamation marks, no stacked superlatives.
- Promise outcomes that aren't in a policy someone has actually written.
- Invent a student, a quote, a bio detail, or a guarantee term. Every
  testimonial on the site is a real Google review.

## Scope

reduced-motion-friendly delivery.

declined); referral/promo codes.

photography, dedicated testimonials, package inclusions, cancellation wording,
and the confidence-guarantee terms. All tracked in `07-status.md`.
